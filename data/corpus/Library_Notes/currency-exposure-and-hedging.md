# Currency exposure and hedging for Kiwi investors

## Where currency exposure comes from
When a New Zealand investor buys an unhedged global or US share fund, they own assets priced in foreign currencies, mostly US dollars. Their NZD return then has two parts: the change in the share prices, and the change in the exchange rate.

## How it moves returns
If the NZD falls against the USD, the same US shares are worth more NZD, which adds to returns. If the NZD rises, it subtracts. Example: USD 10,000 of shares bought when NZD 1 = USD 0.60 cost NZD 16,667. If the shares are unchanged but the rate moves to 0.55, they are worth NZD 18,182 (a 9% gain from currency alone). If it moves to 0.65, they are worth NZD 15,385 (an 8% loss).

## Currency as a partial cushion
The NZD has historically tended to fall during global market stress. For NZD-based investors, that has often softened share-market falls in unhedged offshore holdings. This pattern is not guaranteed.

## Hedging
A hedged fund uses currency contracts to remove most of the exchange-rate effect, so returns track the underlying assets in local currency. Hedging has a cost or benefit roughly equal to the interest-rate difference between the two currencies, and it removes the cushioning effect above. Many NZ fund managers hedge bonds almost fully and hedge shares partly. There is no single right hedge ratio; it depends on when the money will be spent and in which currency.

## Costs of converting money
Platforms and brokers charge to convert NZD to USD, often as a margin on the exchange rate. For regular small purchases this can cost more each year than the ETF's own fee. Compare conversion costs before choosing a platform.
