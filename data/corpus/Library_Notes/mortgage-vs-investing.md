# Paying off the mortgage versus investing

## The basic comparison
Every extra dollar paid off a home loan earns a return equal to the mortgage interest rate, with certainty. In New Zealand, interest on an owner-occupied home is not tax-deductible, and the saving is not taxed, so it is an after-tax, risk-free return. Investing the same dollar has a higher expected return over long periods but can lose money, and the returns are taxed (for example PIE tax in managed funds, or FIF income on overseas shares).

A fair comparison uses after-tax, after-fee expected investment returns against the mortgage rate. If a mortgage costs 6% and a growth portfolio is expected to earn 7% before fees and tax, the expected advantage of investing after costs may be small, and it comes with risk.

## Factors people weigh
- Interest rate and term: higher mortgage rates make repayment more attractive.
- Time horizon: investing has more time to outperform over 20 years than over 5.
- Risk tolerance and security: owning a home outright reduces fixed costs in retirement and during job loss.
- KiwiSaver employer contributions and the government contribution: these are extra money that repayment cannot match, so many people contribute at least enough to receive them.
- Flexibility: money in a revolving credit or offset facility can be redrawn; extra lump-sum repayments on a fixed loan may incur break fees.
- Diversification: someone with most of their wealth in one house may value building assets elsewhere.

## A middle path
Many households split surplus money between the two, or prioritise paying down the mortgage before retirement while keeping contributions going.
