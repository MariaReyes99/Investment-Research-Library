# Safe withdrawal rates and the 4% rule

## Where the 4% rule comes from
In 1994 financial planner William Bengen studied US market history from 1926 onwards. He asked: if a retiree withdraws a fixed percentage of their starting portfolio in year one, then raises that dollar amount with inflation each year, what is the highest starting rate that never ran out of money over 30 years? For portfolios holding roughly 50–75% shares, the answer was about 4%. A 1998 study by three professors at Trinity University (the "Trinity study") reached similar conclusions.

## What the rule means in practice
A 4% withdrawal rate implies needing about 25 times the yearly income you want from the portfolio. NZD 40,000 a year → about NZD 1,000,000. NZD 60,000 a year → about NZD 1,500,000. The library's calculator uses this relationship when a goal is set as a yearly income.

## Limitations
- It is based on US history, one of the strongest-performing markets. Studies of other countries have found lower safe rates.
- It assumes a 30-year retirement. Early retirees (the FIRE community) planning for 40–50 years often use 3–3.5%.
- It ignores fees. A 1% fee effectively turns a 4% plan into a 5% withdrawal.
- It assumes rigid spending. Retirees who cut spending after bad years can usually start higher.

## New Zealand context
NZ Superannuation is paid from age 65 to eligible residents regardless of other income. The portfolio only needs to fund the gap between NZ Super and desired spending, which lowers the capital required. Check current NZ Super rates with Work and Income.
