# ETF domicile: US-listed versus Irish-domiciled ETFs for NZ investors

## What domicile means
An ETF's domicile is the country where the fund is legally set up. Two funds can track the same index but be domiciled in different countries, for example iShares Core S&P 500 ETF (IVV, US-domiciled) and iShares Core S&P 500 UCITS ETF (CSPX, Irish-domiciled, listed on the London Stock Exchange). Domicile affects estate tax, withholding tax on dividends, and whether dividends are paid out.

## US estate tax
Shares in US-domiciled funds are US assets. When a non-US person dies holding US assets above a small exemption (US$60,000 for most non-residents), US estate tax can apply to the excess unless a tax treaty gives more relief. Australia has an estate tax treaty with the US; New Zealand does not. Irish-domiciled funds are not US assets, so this exposure does not arise. This is the main reason many NZ investors with larger portfolios prefer Irish-domiciled funds such as CSPX or IWDA.

## Dividends and withholding tax
US dividends paid to a fund or investor are subject to US withholding tax, usually 15% under a tax treaty once the correct form (W-8BEN) is filed. With a US-domiciled ETF the withholding happens when the ETF pays the investor. With an Irish-domiciled fund, the fund itself suffers the withholding on US shares it holds. How foreign tax credits then work in New Zealand differs between the two; an accountant can confirm the effect for a given situation.

## Accumulating versus distributing
Many Irish-domiciled ETFs come in "Acc" (accumulating) versions that reinvest dividends inside the fund, and "Dist" versions that pay them out. Most US ETFs distribute. For NZ investors above the FIF de minimis threshold, tax is generally based on FIF rules rather than on dividends received, so the choice mostly affects convenience and cash flow.

## Other practical differences
Irish-domiciled funds usually trade in London in USD or GBP and need a broker with access to the London Stock Exchange. Trading volumes, fees and spreads differ. NZ-domiciled PIE funds that hold overseas shares are a third option: the fund handles the tax, and they do not count towards an individual's FIF threshold.
