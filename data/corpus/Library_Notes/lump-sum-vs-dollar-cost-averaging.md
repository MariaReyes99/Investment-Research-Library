# Lump sum versus dollar-cost averaging

## The two approaches
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, for example monthly. Most people do this automatically through salary contributions. The debate is about a lump sum already in hand, such as an inheritance or house sale proceeds: invest it all now, or feed it in over months?

## What the research shows
Because markets rise more often than they fall, investing a lump sum immediately has historically produced a higher ending value more often than spreading it out. Vanguard research published in 2012, covering the US, UK and Australian markets, found lump-sum investing beat a 12-month DCA plan about two-thirds of the time.

## Why people still choose to spread it
DCA reduces the regret of investing everything just before a fall. For someone who would panic and sell after an early loss, a plan they can stick to may beat the theoretically better one. Spreading money over a defined, short period (for example 6–12 months) with a fixed schedule avoids the bigger risk: holding cash indefinitely while waiting for the "right time".

## Questions to think through
- How would you feel, and what would you do, if the market fell 20% the month after you invested?
- Is the money needed within a few years? If so, the question is asset allocation, not timing.
- Are there costs per transaction that make many small purchases expensive?
