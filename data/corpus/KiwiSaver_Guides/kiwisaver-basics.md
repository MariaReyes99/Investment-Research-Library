# KiwiSaver basics (as at September 2026)

## What KiwiSaver is
KiwiSaver is New Zealand's voluntary, work-based retirement savings scheme. Money is invested in a fund chosen by the member (or a default fund) and is generally available from age 65, or earlier for a first home purchase after three years of membership, significant financial hardship, serious illness or permanent emigration (except to Australia).

## Contributions
- Employee rates: 3.5%, 4%, 6%, 8% or 10% of gross pay. The default rate rose from 3% to 3.5% on 1 April 2026 and is scheduled to rise to 4% on 1 April 2028. Members can apply for a temporary rate reduction to stay at 3%.
- Employer contributions: at least 3.5% of gross pay from 1 April 2026 (rising to 4% from 1 April 2028), subject to employer superannuation contribution tax (ESCT). Employers must also contribute for employed 16- and 17-year-olds from 1 April 2026.
- Government contribution: since 1 July 2025, 25 cents for every dollar a member contributes, up to NZD 260.72 a year. To get the full amount, a member needs to contribute at least NZD 1,042.86 between 1 July and 30 June. Members with income over NZD 180,000 are not eligible; 16- and 17-year-olds are.

These settings were changed in Budgets 2025 and 2026. Check IRD for the current rules.

## Fund types
Providers offer funds ranging from defensive and conservative (mostly bonds and cash) through balanced to growth and aggressive (mostly shares). Growth funds have higher expected long-term returns and larger short-term falls. The right type depends on when the money will be needed. Members can switch funds or providers.

## Tax
KiwiSaver funds are usually portfolio investment entities (PIEs). Returns are taxed inside the fund at the member's prescribed investor rate (PIR): 10.5%, 17.5% or 28%, based on income. Using the wrong PIR can lead to overpaying, or to a tax bill if it is too low.

## Fees
Fees vary widely between providers and funds and have a large effect over decades. Compare fees and returns on Sorted Smart Investor.
