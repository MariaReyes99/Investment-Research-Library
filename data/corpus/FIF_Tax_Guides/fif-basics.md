# Foreign investment fund (FIF) tax basics (as at September 2026)

## What the FIF rules cover
New Zealand's foreign investment fund (FIF) rules tax NZ tax residents on many offshore investments held directly, such as US-listed ETFs, Irish-domiciled ETFs and shares in overseas companies. Tax can be due each year even if nothing was sold and no dividend was paid.

## The de minimis threshold
Individuals whose FIF investments cost NZD 50,000 or less in total, at all times during the tax year (1 April to 31 March), do not have to calculate FIF income. Instead they return actual dividends received.
- The test uses cost (what was paid, in NZD), not current market value.
- It applies per individual, across all brokers and platforms combined.
- If cost exceeds the threshold at any time in the year, even for a day, the FIF rules apply to the whole overseas portfolio for that year, not just the amount above the threshold.
- It does not apply to companies or most trusts.

Proposed change: Budget 2026 (28 May 2026) proposed raising the threshold to NZD 100,000 from 1 April 2026 and extending a realisation-based method (dividends plus 70% of realised gains, less 70% of realised losses) to all taxpayers. At the time this note was written these were proposals subject to legislation. Check IRD for whether they have passed and which year they apply to.

## Calculation methods
- Fair dividend rate (FDR): FIF income is 5% of the portfolio's market value on 1 April, plus a "quick sale adjustment" for investments bought and sold within the same year. It applies regardless of actual returns.
- Comparative value (CV): FIF income is the actual gain over the year (closing value plus sale proceeds and dividends, minus opening value and purchases). If the portfolio falls, CV income can be zero.
- Individuals and family trusts using FDR can usually choose CV instead in years when it gives a lower result, which caps FIF income at 5% but lets it fall to zero in a bad year.

## Exemptions and alternatives
- Shares in certain ASX-listed Australian companies that maintain franking accounts are exempt, and do not count towards the threshold.
- NZ-domiciled PIE funds (including KiwiSaver funds and NZ-listed funds that invest offshore) deal with FIF tax inside the fund; they do not count towards an individual's threshold.
