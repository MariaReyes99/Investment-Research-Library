# Australian ETFs: structure, costs and risks

## What an ETF represents
An exchange-traded fund is a managed fund traded on an exchange. Investors own units in the fund rather than the underlying assets directly. ETFs can hold Australian or international shares, bonds, cash, property, commodities or other exposures. Unit prices and distributions can rise or fall.

## How strategies differ
Passive ETFs generally seek to track an index or asset. Active ETFs use a manager to select investments for a stated objective. Some funds use derivatives, borrowing or short selling; these strategies can add risks beyond ordinary market movements. Read the fund's current product disclosure statement to understand its strategy and risks.

## Costs to compare
Consider the ongoing management cost, brokerage, transaction costs, bid-ask spread, currency conversion and any platform or account fee. A low management fee does not necessarily mean the lowest total cost for a particular investor or trading pattern.

## Risks and comparison
Risks can include market, sector, currency, liquidity, interest-rate, credit, manager and complex-strategy risk. Compare funds with similar objectives and underlying exposures, and check the date and contents of the PDS. Historical returns are not predictions.
